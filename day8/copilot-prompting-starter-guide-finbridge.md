# Microsoft 365 Copilot Prompting Starter Guide
### For FinBridge Office Staff

---

## Welcome

Microsoft 365 Copilot is built into the apps you already use every day — Outlook, Word, and Excel. It can save you real time on drafting, summarising, and organising work.

The good news: you don't need to be technical to get great results. You just need to know how to ask well.

A quick word you'll see throughout this guide: **prompt**. A prompt is simply what you type to Copilot — your instruction or question. That's all it is. There's no special language to learn.

This guide gives you a simple formula to remember, plus ready-to-use examples you can copy and adapt today.

---

## The Good Prompt Formula

A prompt that works well usually answers four short questions:

> **Who am I? | What do I want? | What should it include? | What tone or format?**

You don't need all four every time — but the more context you give, the better what Copilot writes for you.

**Example of a weak prompt:**
> *Write an email about the meeting.*

**Example of a stronger prompt using the formula:**
> *I'm a finance administrator. Write a polite email to a colleague reminding them that the monthly budget sign-off is due this Friday. Keep it brief and friendly.*

Think of it like briefing a capable colleague who has just joined the team — they need the context you already have in your head.

## Outlook Examples

### 1. Drafting a follow-up email

> *I'm following up after a project catch-up meeting. Draft a short, professional email to the team summarising that we agreed to move the deadline to 20 August, that Sarah will lead on the client report, and that we'll meet again next Thursday. Friendly but concise.*

### 2. Summarising a long email thread

> *Summarise this email thread in 3 bullet points. Tell me: what is being asked, who needs to act, and what the deadline is.*
>
> *(Type this, press Enter, then paste the email thread underneath it. If the thread mentions specific client names or account numbers, remove those before pasting — there's a simple guide to this at the end of the document.)*

### 3. Replying to a complaint politely

> *A customer has emailed to say their invoice was incorrect and they are frustrated. Draft a calm, apologetic reply acknowledging the error, letting them know we are investigating, and saying we will respond within 2 business days. Professional and empathetic tone.*

## Word Examples

### 1. Drafting a team update

> *Write a one-page team update for our department. Include three sections: what we completed this month, what is in progress, and what is coming up next month. Use plain, clear language. I'll fill in the specific details — just set up the structure and some placeholder text.*

### 2. Improving a draft you've already written

> *I've written the paragraph below. Please rewrite it to be clearer and more professional, without changing the meaning. Remove anything that isn't necessary.*
>
> *(Then paste your paragraph underneath and press Enter.)*

### 3. Creating a meeting agenda

> *Create a meeting agenda for a 45-minute team meeting. The topics are: project status update (10 mins), budget review (15 mins), upcoming office move (10 mins), and any other business (10 mins). Include a space for the date, attendees, and action points at the end.*

## Excel Examples

### 1. Writing a formula in plain English

> *I have a spreadsheet tracking monthly expenses. Column A has the category name, column B has the amount spent. I want a formula in column C that shows the word "Over budget" if the amount in column B is more than 500, and "OK" if it is 500 or under. How do I write that?*

### 2. Explaining what a formula does

If you've inherited a spreadsheet and have no idea what a formula means — that's exactly what this is for. Just paste the formula in and ask.

> *Can you explain what this Excel formula does in plain English?*
>
> *(Paste the formula here — for example:* `=SUMIF(A2:A100,"Travel",B2:B100)`*)*

You don't need to understand the formula yourself. That's the point.

### 3. Structuring a tracker

> *Help me set up a simple project task tracker in Excel. I need columns to track: task name, person responsible, due date, status (Not started / In progress / Done), and any notes. Suggest the column headers and any formatting tips to make it easy to read.*

## Tips for Better Results

- **Be specific.** Vague questions get vague answers. The more detail you give, the better what Copilot writes will be.
- **Say what format you want.** "Give me bullet points" or "write this as a short paragraph" makes a real difference.
- **The first attempt is just a starting point.** If you're not happy with what it wrote, just type a follow-up — for example: *"make it shorter"*, *"make it more formal"*, or *"add a sentence at the end thanking them"*. You don't need to start again.
- **You always have the final say.** Copilot drafts; you decide. Always read what it produces before you send or save anything.

## A Note on Data Safety

**The short version: using Copilot on your FinBridge work account is safe and approved. Go ahead and use it.**

Microsoft 365 Copilot — the version built into your work Outlook, Word, and Excel — runs inside FinBridge's own Microsoft environment. It is covered by the same security and privacy controls as the rest of your work tools. You do not need to worry about using it for everyday work tasks.

The one habit to keep (and this was covered on Day 1 of this programme):

> **If you ever use a free AI tool on the internet — a public chatbot, a website, or anything outside your work account — do not paste client names, account numbers, or personal details into it.**

Think of it the same way you'd think about forwarding a work email to your personal Gmail — fine for general things, but not for anything that belongs to a client or colleague.

**Simple rule:**
- Copilot on your **work account** (Outlook, Word, Excel) = go ahead.
- Any **external or personal AI tool** = keep client details out of it.

If you're ever unsure, just ask your line manager or the IT helpdesk — there's no such thing as a silly question on this one.

## Quick Reference Card

| I want to… | Try saying… |
|---|---|
| Draft an email | *"Write a [tone] email to [audience] about [topic]. Include [key points]."* |
| Summarise something | *"Summarise this in [X] bullet points. Focus on [what matters]."* |
| Improve my writing | *"Rewrite this to be clearer and more professional. Keep the meaning."* |
| Build a document structure | *"Create a template for [document type] with sections for [topics]."* |
| Understand a formula | *"Explain what this formula does in plain English: [formula]."* |
| Write a formula | *"I need a formula that [what you want it to do], using columns [X and Y]."* |

*The words in [square brackets] are placeholders — replace them with your own details. For example, replace [tone] with "friendly" or "formal".*
